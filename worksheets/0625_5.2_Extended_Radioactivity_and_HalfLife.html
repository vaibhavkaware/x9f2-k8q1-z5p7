Section A: multiple choice

One mark each. Select an answer to check it.

1

The emission of radiation from a nucleus is described as a random process.

What is meant by random in this description?

Mark scheme

Answer A

Explanation Random means that the moment at which any one nucleus decays cannot be predicted, and nor can the direction of the emission. Only the behaviour of a very large number of nuclei is predictable, which is why half-life is a reliable quantity.

Connection Randomness is the reason count-rate readings fluctuate and never lie exactly on a smooth decay curve.

Common mistake Choosing B. That statement is true, but it describes the word spontaneous, not random. Candidates regularly swap the two terms.

2

Which row describes the nature of the three types of nuclear emission?

α-particleβ-particleγ-radiation
Ahelium nucleuselectron from an orbit around the nucleuselectromagnetic wave
Bhydrogen nucleuselectron emitted from the nucleuselectromagnetic wave
Chelium nucleuselectron emitted from the nucleuselectromagnetic wave
Dhelium nucleuselectron emitted from the nucleushigh-speed neutron
Mark scheme

Answer C

Explanation An α-particle is two protons and two neutrons, which is a helium nucleus. A β-particle is a high-speed electron that is emitted from the nucleus. γ-radiation is an electromagnetic wave.

Connection The nature fixes the charge (+2, –1, 0), and the charge decides how each emission behaves in electric and magnetic fields.

Common mistake Choosing A. The β-particle is not one of the orbiting electrons; it is created in the nucleus when a neutron changes into a proton and an electron.

3

A radioactive source is placed 2 cm from a detector. Different absorbers are placed, one at a time, between the source and the detector.

The background count rate is 20 counts / min. The table shows the count rate measured with each absorber.

absorbercount rate / counts per min
none480
sheet of paper478
3 mm of aluminium160
2 cm of lead28

Which types of radiation does the source emit?

Mark scheme

Answer D

Explanation Paper makes no real difference (480 to 478 is within random variation), so there is no α. Aluminium removes a large part of the count, so β is present. The count is still well above background after the aluminium and only lead brings it close to background, so γ is present.

Connection Compare every reading with the background count rate, not with zero. A reading of 28 against a background of 20 means almost nothing from the source is getting through.

Common mistake Choosing C because three absorbers are listed. The absorbers are tests; a type of radiation is present only if its absorber causes a clear drop.

4

Which list gives the three types of nuclear emission in order of ionising effect, from the most ionising to the least ionising?

Mark scheme

Answer B

Explanation α is the most strongly ionising, β is less ionising and γ is the least ionising.

Connection The order of penetrating ability is the exact reverse: an emission that ionises strongly loses its energy quickly and so does not travel far.

Common mistake Choosing C by confusing ionising effect with penetrating ability.

5

Why is an α-particle more strongly ionising than a β-particle?

Mark scheme

Answer C

Explanation The α-particle has a relative charge of +2 and the β-particle –1, so the α-particle exerts a larger force on the electrons of the atoms it passes. It also has a greater kinetic energy, so it can ionise many atoms before it stops.

Connection This is the Supplement explanation: the syllabus asks for both electric charge and kinetic energy.

Common mistake Choosing D. An α-particle travels only a few centimetres in air, which is a result of its strong ionising effect and not a cause of it.

6

A beam of radiation contains α-particles, β-particles and γ-rays. The beam passes, in a vacuum, between two charged metal plates. The diagram shows the three paths P, Q and R.

Which row identifies the radiation following each path?

PQR
Aαγβ
Bβγα
Cβαγ
Dγβα
Mark scheme

Answer B

Explanation γ-rays have no charge and are not deflected, so Q is γ. β-particles are negative and are attracted to the positive plate, so P is β. α-particles are positive and move towards the negative plate, so R is α. The β path curves more because a β-particle has a much smaller mass.

Connection Unlike charges attract and like charges repel (topic 4.2.1). The same reasoning is used for any charged particle in an electric field.

Common mistake Choosing A by expecting the α-particle, which has the larger charge, to be deflected more. Its mass is thousands of times greater than that of a β-particle, so its path curves much less.

7

A beam of radiation travels from left to right, in a vacuum, and enters a region where there is a uniform magnetic field directed into the page.

The beam contains α-particles and β-particles.

Which row gives the direction of the force on each type of particle as it enters the field?

force on α-particleforce on β-particle
Ainto the pageout of the page
Btowards the bottom of the pagetowards the top of the page
Cto the rightto the left
Dtowards the top of the pagetowards the bottom of the page
Mark scheme

Answer D

Explanation A beam of α-particles moving to the right is a conventional current to the right. With the field into the page, the left-hand rule gives a force towards the top of the page. β-particles are negative, so their conventional current is to the left and the force is towards the bottom of the page.

Connection This is the left-hand rule for the force on a beam of charged particles (topic 4.5.4). The force is always at right angles to both the field and the motion.

Common mistake Choosing A. Charged particles are not pushed along the magnetic field lines; that would be the behaviour in an electric field.

8

A radioactive isotope has a half-life of 6.0 hours. A sample contains 8.0 × 106 nuclei of this isotope.

How many nuclei of the isotope remain undecayed after 18 hours?

Mark scheme

Answer A

Explanation 18 hours is 18 ÷ 6.0 = 3 half-lives. 8.0 × 106 → 4.0 × 106 → 2.0 × 106 → 1.0 × 106.

Connection The number of undecayed nuclei, the activity and the corrected count rate all halve in one half-life.

Common mistake Choosing C by dividing by 3 instead of halving three times, or D by giving the number that have decayed.

9

The activity of a radioactive sample is the number of nuclei in the sample that decay each second. Activity is measured in becquerels (Bq).

The graph shows how the activity of a sample of a radioactive isotope changes with time.

What is the half-life of the isotope?

Mark scheme

Answer B

Explanation The activity starts at 800 Bq. Half of this is 400 Bq, which is reached at 15 min. Checking with a second interval: 400 Bq to 200 Bq takes from 15 min to 30 min, also 15 min.

Connection No background correction is needed because the graph shows activity of the sample, not a count rate measured by a detector.

Common mistake Choosing C by reading the time for the activity to fall to one quarter, or D by reading the end of the time axis.

10

A detector connected to a counter is used to measure the count rate near a radioactive source. The reading is 96 counts / min.

The source is removed. The counter then records 240 counts in 10 minutes.

What is the corrected count rate for the source?

Mark scheme

Answer B

Explanation Background count rate = 240 ÷ 10 = 24 counts / min. Corrected count rate = 96 – 24 = 72 counts / min.

Connection Background is measured over a long time and then divided by the time, because a single short reading of a small, random count is unreliable.

Common mistake Choosing D by adding the background, or A by giving the background count rate itself.

11

The background count rate in a laboratory is 30 counts / min.

A detector near a radioactive source gives a reading of 350 counts / min. Exactly 12 hours later, the reading is 70 counts / min.

What is the half-life of the source?

Mark scheme

Answer C

Explanation Corrected count rates are 350 – 30 = 320 counts / min and 70 – 30 = 40 counts / min. 320 → 160 → 80 → 40 is 3 half-lives in 12 hours, so the half-life is 4.0 h.

Connection Only the part of the reading that comes from the source halves. The background part stays constant.

Common mistake Working with 350 and 70 directly. These are not related by a whole number of halvings, which is the signal that background has not yet been subtracted.

12

A detector is placed close to a radioactive source. The graph shows how the count rate measured by the detector changes with time. Background radiation has not been subtracted from the readings.

The count rate becomes constant at 40 counts / min.

What is the half-life of the source?

Mark scheme

Answer B

Explanation The constant value of 40 counts / min is the background. Corrected count rate at the start = 240 – 40 = 200 counts / min. Half of this is 100 counts / min, which is a reading of 100 + 40 = 140 counts / min. The graph reaches 140 counts / min at 3.0 h.

Connection Add the background back before reading the graph, because the graph shows uncorrected readings.

Common mistake Choosing C by halving the uncorrected reading (240 → 120) and reading the time at 120 counts / min. Ignoring background always gives a half-life that is too long.

13

A radioactive isotope has a half-life of 12 minutes.

What fraction of the nuclei in a sample of the isotope has decayed after 36 minutes?

Mark scheme

Answer D

Explanation 36 minutes is 3 half-lives. The fraction remaining is ½ × ½ × ½ = 1/8, so the fraction that has decayed is 1 – 1/8 = 7/8.

Connection Read the question for remaining or decayed. The two fractions always add up to 1.

Common mistake Choosing A, which is the fraction remaining.

14

A student heats a sample of a radioactive isotope from 20 °C to 80 °C.

What happens to the half-life of the isotope?

Mark scheme

Answer C

Explanation Radioactive decay is spontaneous. It is a change in the nucleus and is not affected by temperature, pressure or chemical combination, so the half-life is unchanged.

Connection Heating changes the kinetic energy of whole atoms and molecules (topic 2.1.2). It does not change what happens inside the nucleus.

Common mistake Choosing A by treating decay like a chemical reaction or like evaporation, both of which speed up when the temperature rises.

15

A detector is placed 1 cm from a radioactive source in air. The count rate is much greater than the background count rate.

The detector is moved to a distance of 10 cm from the source. The count rate falls to the background count rate.

Which radiation does the source emit?

Mark scheme

Answer A

Explanation α-particles are stopped by a few centimetres of air. β-particles and γ-radiation would still reach a detector 10 cm away, so the source emits only α-particles.

Connection Air is an absorber. The short range of α-particles in air follows from their strong ionising effect.

Common mistake Assuming that any fall in count rate with distance is just the radiation spreading out. Spreading out reduces the count rate; it does not reduce it to background within 10 cm.
