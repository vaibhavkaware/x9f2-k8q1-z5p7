1

In the experiment that gave evidence for the nuclear model of the atom, particles were directed at a target.

Which row describes the particles and the target?

particlestarget
Aα-particlesthin metal foil
Bβ-particlesthin metal foil
Cα-particlesthick block of lead
Dneutronsthin metal foil
Mark scheme

Answer A

Explanation α-particles were directed at a very thin sheet of metal (gold foil) and the directions in which they left the foil were recorded.

Connection The syllabus wording is the scattering of α-particles by a sheet of thin metal. Gold is the usual example, not a requirement.

Common mistake Choosing C. A thick block would absorb every α-particle, so no scattering pattern could be observed.

2

In the α-particle scattering experiment, most of the α-particles pass straight through the metal foil without being deflected.

What does this observation show about the atom?

Mark scheme

Answer C

Explanation An α-particle that is not deflected has not passed close to anything that can exert a large force on it. As this is true for most α-particles, most of the atom must be empty space.

Connection Each of the three conclusions about the nucleus is tied to one particular observation. Learn them as pairs.

Common mistake Choosing A or B. Those statements are true, but they come from the α-particles that are deflected, not from the ones that pass straight through.

3

Which observation from the α-particle scattering experiment shows that the nucleus is very small compared with the atom?

Mark scheme

Answer B

Explanation Only an α-particle that passes very close to a nucleus is deflected through a large angle. Because this happens to very few of them, the nucleus must be a very small target.

Connection A small proportion of large deflections means a small nucleus. A large proportion of straight-through paths means mostly empty space. They are two sides of the same result.

Common mistake Choosing C. Repulsion shows the sign of the charge on the nucleus, not its size.

4

The diagram shows a gold nucleus and four paths, A, B, C and D, for α-particles approaching the nucleus.

Which path is not possible?

Mark scheme

Answer D

Explanation The α-particle and the gold nucleus are both positively charged, so the force on the α-particle is always a repulsion, directed away from the nucleus. Path D curves towards the nucleus, which would need an attractive force.

Connection A is a large deflection for a close approach, B is a head-on approach that returns along its path, and C is a small deflection for a distant approach. All three are repulsions.

Common mistake Choosing B. A path that reverses looks impossible, but it is the most important observation in the experiment.

5

Some of the α-particles in the scattering experiment are deflected away from the nuclei of the metal atoms.

What is the reason for this?

Mark scheme

Answer B

Explanation Like charges repel. An α-particle has a relative charge of +2, so a repulsive force shows that the nucleus is also positively charged.

Connection This is the electrostatic force between charges from topic 4.2.1 acting on the scale of the atom.

Common mistake Choosing D. Electrons have far too little mass to change the path of an α-particle noticeably.

6

Why is the α-particle scattering experiment carried out in a vacuum?

Mark scheme

Answer A

Explanation α-particles are strongly ionising and are stopped by a few centimetres of air. Without a vacuum they would not reach the foil or the detector.

Connection This uses the penetrating ability of α-particles from topic 5.2.2.

Common mistake Choosing B. The speed of the α-particles is not the issue; whether they arrive at all is.

7

Why must the metal foil used in the α-particle scattering experiment be very thin?

Mark scheme

Answer D

Explanation α-particles have a very small penetrating ability. A foil only a few hundred atoms thick lets them pass through, and it also means that an α-particle is unlikely to be deflected by more than one nucleus.

Connection Gold is chosen in practice because it can be beaten into an extremely thin foil.

Common mistake Choosing C. The whole point of the experiment is that the α-particles leave in different directions.

8

The diagram shows the apparatus for an α-particle scattering experiment. The detector can be moved to positions X, Y and Z.

Which row describes the number of α-particles detected each minute at the three positions?

XYZ
Alargelargezero
Bvery largesmallvery small
Csmallsmallsmall
Dzerosmallvery large
Mark scheme

Answer B

Explanation Most α-particles pass straight through to X. Some are deflected through small angles, so a small number reach Y. Very few, but not zero, are deflected through more than 90° and reach Z.

Connection The numbers fall very steeply as the angle of deflection increases, which is the evidence for a very small nucleus.

Common mistake Choosing A. The count at Z is tiny but it is not zero, and that non-zero count is what overturned the earlier model of the atom.

9

Which statement is not a conclusion that can be drawn from the α-particle scattering experiment?

Mark scheme

Answer C

Explanation The experiment shows that the nucleus is very small, positively charged and contains most of the mass of the atom. It gives no information about what the nucleus is made of.

Connection The composition of the nucleus in terms of protons and neutrons is topic 5.1.2, which rests on different evidence.

Common mistake Rejecting C because it is a true statement. The question asks what this experiment shows, not what is true about atoms.

10

Which observation gives evidence that the nucleus contains most of the mass of the atom?

Mark scheme

Answer A

Explanation An α-particle can be turned back only by something much more massive than itself. If the nucleus had a small mass, the nucleus would be pushed aside and the α-particle would carry on forwards.

Connection Compare a ball bouncing back from a wall with a ball hitting a much lighter ball (momentum, topic 1.6).

Common mistake Choosing B. Passing straight through is evidence for empty space and says nothing about where the mass is.

11

In an earlier model of the atom, the positive charge and the mass were spread evenly throughout the whole atom.

What would be observed in the α-particle scattering experiment if this earlier model were correct?

Mark scheme

Answer C

Explanation Charge and mass that are spread out could exert only weak forces on an α-particle, so every α-particle would pass through almost undeflected. Large deflections need the charge and mass to be concentrated in a very small region.

Connection This is how a model is tested: it makes a prediction, and the prediction is compared with the observations.

Common mistake Choosing A by picturing the earlier atom as a solid object that blocks the α-particles.
